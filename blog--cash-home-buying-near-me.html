What Is Cash Home Buying and How Does It Work?

Cash home buying is a straightforward transaction where a buyer purchases your property with liquid funds - no mortgage financing required. The process eliminates the delays and contingencies that come with traditional bank loans, making it attractive for homeowners facing foreclosure, relocation deadlines, inherited properties, or homes needing significant repairs.

Here in Middletown, OH, the cash sale process typically unfolds in four steps. First, you contact a cash buyer and request an offer. Second, they conduct a brief inspection (usually 1–2 days). Third, they present a written offer based on the property's condition and local market value. Fourth, you close and receive funds - often within 7–14 days.

The speed differential is substantial. According to NAR data, cash sales close in a median of 7–14 days for local investors, compared to approximately 52 days for financed buyer closings. This timeline advantage alone makes cash sales compelling for sellers under time pressure.

Three main types of cash buyers operate in the Middletown market:

  • Local real estate investors - individuals or small companies who purchase distressed properties, renovate them, and resell or rent them.
  • iBuyers - national platforms that make instant offers and handle closing, though coverage in secondary markets like Middletown is limited.
  • House-flipping companies - franchised operations that specialize in quick acquisitions and renovations.

Each buyer type has different offer ranges, closing timelines, and fee structures - a distinction that directly affects your net proceeds.

A national platform might offer higher before fees, but after service charges, net proceeds vary. The spread is real, which is why shopping multiple offers is critical.

Why are cash offers lower?

Cash buyers account for several costs that traditional buyers don't:

  • Repair and renovation expenses (often $15,000–$50,000 for distressed homes)
  • Holding costs (property taxes, insurance, utilities while renovating)
  • Closing costs and title insurance
  • Profit margin (typically 15–25% of the final resale price)

The investor 70% rule formula captures this: Maximum Allowable Offer = ARV × 0.70 − Estimated Repair Costs. If your $171,400 home needs $20,000 in repairs, the calculation is ($171,400 × 0.70) − $20,000 = $100,000. This is the investor's maximum offer to maintain their profit target.

How to Get the Best Cash Offer for Your Home

Accepting the first offer is a costly mistake. Strategic negotiation can recover thousands of dollars.

Five tactics to maximize your offer:

  1. Obtain minimum three competing offers. Different buyers apply different profit margins and repair estimates. A $171,400 home might receive offers of $115,000, $128,000, and $135,000 - a $20,000 spread from shopping around. Request offers in writing and give yourself 48 hours to compare before accepting.
  2. Know your ARV before calls. Use Zillow, Redfin, or the Butler County Auditor's recent sales data to estimate your home's after-repair value. If a buyer's offer seems to underestimate ARV, you have data to push back.
  3. Disclose repairs but don't over-explain. Provide a list of needed repairs (roof, HVAC, foundation, etc.) with rough cost estimates. Don't volunteer additional problems or exaggerate severity - let the buyer's inspector verify.
  4. Ask for earnest money. Cash buyers typically offer $500–$1,000 earnest money, far below the 1–3% standard in financed deals. Negotiate for higher earnest money ($2,000–$5,000) to ensure the buyer is serious and to protect yourself if they back out.
  5. Negotiate closing date flexibility. If you need to close in 7 days but a buyer prefers 14, ask if they'll increase the offer by $2,000–$5,000 in exchange for a longer timeline. Many investors will accept this trade-off.

Real example: A $220,000 ARV home in Middletown receives three offers:

  • Offer A: $154,000 (70% ARV)
  • Offer B: $168,000 (76% ARV)
  • Offer C: $176,000 (80% ARV)

By shopping around, you recover $22,000 more than the first offer - a 14% improvement.

Documents to prepare before requesting offers:

  • Title deed or mortgage statement
  • Utility bills (to verify occupancy)
  • Any repair estimates or inspection reports
  • Photos of major issues (foundation cracks, roof damage, etc.)
  • HOA documents (if applicable)

Having these ready accelerates the offer process and demonstrates professionalism.

What to Expect at Closing When Selling for Cash

The closing process for a cash sale is simpler than a financed transaction but still requires attention to detail.

Step-by-step timeline:

  1. Title search (3–5 business days): The title company searches county records to confirm you own the property and identify any liens, unpaid taxes, or other claims. In Middletown, older homes (many built pre-1970) sometimes have title complications that extend this phase.
  2. Inspection and appraisal (1–2 days): The buyer's inspector visits and documents the property's condition. This is not a contingency in a cash sale - the buyer has already factored repairs into their offer.
  3. Final walkthrough (1 day): You and the buyer confirm the property condition matches the inspection report.
  4. Closing document preparation (2–3 days): The title company prepares the deed, settlement statement, and other closing documents. A proper settlement statement showing the full financial breakdown of the transaction is a standard component of any legitimate cash closing - request one if it isn't provided automatically.
  5. Signing and fund disbursement (1 day): You sign documents at the title company or attorney's office. Funds are wired to your account within 24 hours of signing.

Total timeline: 7–14 days for a straightforward cash sale.

Who pays closing costs?

In cash sales, the buyer often covers closing costs as part of the negotiation. However, this is not automatic - confirm in writing before closing. Typical closing costs in Ohio range from $1,500–$3,000 depending on the home's value and any title issues.

How you receive funds:

Cash buyers wire funds directly to your bank account or to your attorney's trust account (if you've hired legal counsel). Wire transfers typically clear within 24 hours. Confirm the wire instructions in writing and verify the receiving bank account before signing closing documents.

Tax considerations:

Consult a CPA before closing. If you've owned the home as your primary residence for at least 2 of the last 5 years, you may exclude up to $250,000 in capital gains from federal tax (or $500,000 if married filing jointly). Ohio also levies state income tax on capital gains not excluded federally. A CPA can help you understand your specific liability and plan accordingly.

How fast can a cash home buyer close near me?

Direct Answer: Local investors in Middletown typically close in 7–14 days; national platforms close in 14–30 days; house-flipping companies close in 10–21 days.

The speed advantage of cash sales is one of their primary benefits. A 7-day close is realistic for a straightforward transaction with no title complications. However, if your home has liens, unpaid taxes, or probate issues, the timeline may extend to 3–4 weeks.

Do I need to make repairs before selling to a cash buyer?

Direct Answer: No. Cash buyers purchase homes in any condition and factor repair costs into their offer. You should not invest in repairs before selling to a cash buyer.

The buyer's offer already reflects the cost of repairs. If you make repairs before selling, you'll recover only a fraction of that investment in the offer price. The exception is if a repair is critical to safety or title (e.g., unpermitted electrical work) - in that case, disclose it and let the buyer decide whether to proceed.

What are the hidden fees when selling your house for cash?

Direct Answer: Local investors typically charge no separate fees; the discount is built into the offer. Always request a written breakdown of all fees before accepting an offer.

Some buyers attempt to deduct closing costs, title insurance, or inspections at the last moment. Prevent this by negotiating these details in writing before signing the purchase agreement. Confirm who pays closing costs, title insurance, and any other expenses upfront.

Is selling to a cash buyer better than listing with a realtor?

Direct Answer: It depends on your priorities. Cash sales close faster (7–14 days vs. 52 days) but offer lower prices. If speed is critical, cash is better. If price is the priority and you have time, listing on the MLS may net more.

The net proceeds comparison is key. After accounting for agent commissions (5–6%), repairs, and carrying costs in an MLS sale, the difference between a cash offer and traditional listing is often 10–20%. For sellers facing foreclosure, relocation deadlines, or distressed properties, the speed premium justifies the lower price.

Do cash buyers buy homes in any condition?

Direct Answer: Yes. Cash buyers specialize in purchasing homes with deferred maintenance, structural issues, code violations, or other problems that make traditional financing difficult or impossible.

Homes with foundation cracks, roof damage, outdated electrical systems, or significant mold are all candidates for cash sales. The buyer's offer reflects the cost of repairs, so you don't need to fix anything before selling.
